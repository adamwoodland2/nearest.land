    Two modes. Coast snaps your pick to the water's edge and follows each direction across the sea to its first landfall; land within about 20 km in a direction counts as "land in view" and is left blank. Anywhere starts from the exact point you tapped, on land or at sea, and follows each direction to the first land that is not the country you are standing in — so from Berlin, east is Poland at 80 km.

    Not 100% accurate, by nature. The analysis grid is roughly 10 km, so very small islands and narrow straits can be missed, and the exact point you "stand" on is snapped to that grid. Directions are sampled every 1° by default, and one degree of bearing spreads to about 90 km at 5,000 km and 260 km at 15,000 km — so a distant island narrower than that can slip between two bearings. The sampling control above the list tightens that to 0.5° or 0.25° for a little more compute.

    There is also no such thing as the coastline. This is the coastline paradox: the finer you measure a coast, the longer it gets, without limit — every bay contains smaller bays. So any answer depends on the map's level of detail. Here it is Natural Earth's 1:10m outlines (public domain), which is generous for a globe and coarse for a beach.
